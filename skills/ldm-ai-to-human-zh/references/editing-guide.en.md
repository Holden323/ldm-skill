# Chinese AI-to-Human Editing Guide

Load only when systematic review, major rewriting, or justification of changes is needed. The items here are diagnostic signals, not a word blacklist.

## Content layer

### Concrete beats abstract

"Loneliness was deep" has no image; "I scrolled through my contacts and couldn't find a single person I wanted to talk to" can be seen. Prioritize actions, times, places, objects, and outcomes the original already provides. Do not invent facts to manufacture imagery.

### Cut the second explanation

When a story, example, or data already supports the point, the typical AI trace is to add a summary, quip, or elevation. Try deleting the last sentence; if nothing is lost, let the content stop there.

### Don't build the opponent for the reader

"You might think… but actually…" often exists only for the author's convenience in rebutting. When there's no genuine need, state the judgment directly. If the counterpoint is a real question the reader would raise, keep it and respond seriously.

### Don't fabricate the author's identity

AI should not invent "I've experienced this," "A friend of mine," or "I interviewed." Keep reliable source material; without it, convert to unsourced facts or delete.

## Language layer

### Template density

A single "not X, but Y," "you think X, but actually Y," parallelism, rhetorical question, or three-part structure does not need mechanical deletion. Handle only when they appear consecutively, repeat across drafts, or substitute for real argumentation. Common fixes:

- Cut the setup; state the conclusion directly.
- Change the syntax of one instance rather than synonym-swapping every occurrence.
- Merge repeated parallel items, keeping only the two most informative.

### Boilerplate and presentation-speak

Focus on whether words like "furthermore, in fact, it is worth noting that, essentially, at the end of the day, empower, closed loop, lever, granularity" carry real information. Delete if not; keep when genuinely needed in professional context.

### Rhythm

Short-video scripts and social content usually suit short paragraphs with alternating long and short sentences; professional documents can be more complete. Use periods, commas, semicolons, and dashes by meaning and breath, with no blanket bans. Just check whether consecutive sentence lengths and structures are too uniform.

### Address

Consecutive use of "you" can sound like lecturing, but second person itself is not a problem. Adjust among "you," named characters, and neutral statements based on the account persona and platform norms.

## Fact layer

- Data, research, quotes, and thinker attributions must be traceable.
- Vague attribution cannot turn unverified data into reliable fact.
- Precise numbers should be retained only when the source or observation supports them; do not make them more precise on intuition.
- Exaggeration, fiction, and humor should allow the reader to recognize their nature.

## Genre differences

- **Short-video scripts:** Prioritize whether the opening flows, rhythm, and auditory comprehension; some repetition is acceptable.
- **Long-form WeChat articles:** Prioritize argumentation, paragraph progression, and authorial voice; avoid a quotable line in every paragraph.
- **Branded content:** Ensure product information is truthful first, then reduce promotional tone; do not cut necessary compliance language for naturalness.
- **Professional articles:** Terminology accuracy comes first; do not convert all technical terms to colloquial language.

## Review order

Check facts and stance first, then structure and repetition, and only finally word choice and punctuation. Prioritize changes that affect credibility, comprehension, and emotion — do not turn the report into a word-frequency hit list.

## Minimal comparison

Original:

> 事实上，社交媒体的普及并没有让人们更亲近，反而进一步加深了个体的孤独感。

Possible rewrite:

> 社交软件越来越多，想说话时却未必找得到人。

The change works because it removes an information-free opener and turns an abstract judgment into a perceivable situation — not because any word was on a blacklist.
